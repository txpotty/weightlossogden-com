    How Hair Growth Treatment Works in Salt Lake City, UT

    Hair Loss Treatment for Men in Salt Lake City

    Most male hair loss is androgenetic alopecia. DHT, made from testosterone, gradually miniaturizes follicles in genetically sensitive men, starting at the temples or crown. The two main medicines are finasteride, which cuts DHT, and minoxidil, which lengthens the growth phase. Combined use tends to work better than one alone.

    Hair Loss Treatment for Women

    In women, thinning usually shows up as a wider part or less volume on top, while the front hairline stays put. Pattern loss is the usual reason, though low ferritin, thyroid trouble and postpartum shedding can mimic it. Minoxidil is the main FDA-approved option for women. Off-label oral options, such as spironolactone, are added when appropriate.

    Finasteride + Minoxidil in Salt Lake City

    Finasteride is an FDA-approved 1 mg daily tablet for male pattern hair loss, and it lowers scalp and blood DHT by roughly two-thirds. Minoxidil, FDA-approved as a topical, keeps follicles in the growth phase longer so strands come in thicker. The pairing is the standard. Some men use a compounded topical spray that combines both, which is not FDA-approved but may reduce whole-body exposure to finasteride.

    Dutasteride + Minoxidil

    Dutasteride blocks both forms of the enzyme that makes DHT, so it lowers DHT by more than 90 percent in the blood. It is FDA-approved for an enlarged prostate and used off-label for hair loss, most often in men who did not get enough from finasteride after a year. It lingers. Its half-life is about five weeks, so screening and side effect planning take more care. Minoxidil rounds it out.

    Minoxidil + GHK-Cu in Salt Lake City

    Some people want a topical-only plan with no DHT blocker. A compounded minoxidil solution with GHK-Cu, a copper peptide, is one option, with minoxidil doing the main work and GHK-Cu adding early, mostly lab-based support for scalp and follicle health. It is compounded. It is not FDA-approved. It suits women, and men avoiding finasteride.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Treatments

    Your provider weighs sex, age, pattern, speed of loss and medical history. They also ask about pregnancy plans, worries about sexual side effects, past depression and whether you would rather take a pill or apply something to your scalp twice a day. Men with typical pattern loss often start on finasteride and minoxidil. Minoxidil is the usual first step for women.

    What to Expect Over Time in Salt Lake City

    Hair grows about half an inch a month. Visible change takes three to six months, and full results take about a year, so patience is part of the plan. Early shedding on minoxidil is common and usually temporary. Stopping treatment lets the loss resume.

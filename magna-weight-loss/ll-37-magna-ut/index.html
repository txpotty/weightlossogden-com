    How LL-37 Peptide Therapy Works in Magna, UT

    How LL-37 Works in Magna

    LL-37 is cut from a larger protein called hCAP18, and it is the only cathelicidin the human body makes, named for the 2 leucines at its start and its 37 amino acids. In lab studies it punches holes in bacterial and fungal membranes. It also signals other immune cells and has been studied for wound healing. Mostly preclinical. A topical form was tested for venous leg ulcers in early trials, but no LL-37 product is FDA-approved.

    Who Usually Asks About LL-37

    Typical candidates are adults with no inflammatory skin disease who have read about innate immune peptides and want a clinician involved before trying one. A few are already seeing someone for gut or skin concerns. Others use BPC-157 or Thymosin Alpha-1 and want the whole plan reviewed. It is not an antibiotic. An active infection still needs standard treatment.

    Dosing and Schedule in Magna

    Side Effects and Safety in Magna

    Research ties high LL-37 levels to psoriasis and rosacea, and fragments of it can act as a trigger for the immune response in lupus, which is why providers screen so carefully.
